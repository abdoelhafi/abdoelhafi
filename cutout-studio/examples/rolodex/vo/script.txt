[Intrigued, low, documentary narrator] In the 1980s... the most powerful object on a desk wasn't the phone.

[Reveal, a little smile] It was this. The Rolodex. A little wheel of business cards.

[Calm, storytelling] It was invented in 1956 as a simple office tool. Cards on a wheel, A to Z... so a secretary could find a number fast.

[Playful, building] Then Hollywood put one on the desk of every powerful character. And people believed it: a fat Rolodex meant you knew everyone.

[Warm, emphatic] Because every card was a person. A client. A favour. A deal waiting to happen. Your Rolodex was your network. And your network... was your value.

[Slower, a touch of loss] Then the cards moved into phones. The numbers survived. But the story behind each one... where you met, what they needed... mostly got lost.

[Knowing, conversational] Today, you meet more people than ever. And by Friday... you've forgotten who half of them were.

[Quiet, satisfying close] The Rolodex never died. It just needed somewhere to remember.
